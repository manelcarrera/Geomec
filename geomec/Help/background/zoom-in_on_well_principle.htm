Zoom-in on well principles

Within Geomec, a Formation contains a unique material model with a single set of parameters. Material parameters can be varied laterally and with depth (via pointset attribution), but assignment will very much depend on random oriented elements (tetra or hexas) within the Formation. When drilling a well, much more information can be obtained from well logs, usually on a foot/meter scale. This lamination is either difficult to extrapolate to reservoir scale or difficult to mesh in a field scale model.

Within Geomec the sublamination within the Formations is usually neglected, since it complicates the construction of the (gocad) mesh and easily leads to degenerate meshes resulting from the (locally) thin nature of some beds. For large scale processes like subsidence or fault slip, the sublamination is usually of less relevance anyway, contrary to well scale geomechanics like borehole collapse (Stabor) of sand production (Fist) or various fracture prediction programs.

There are four main reasons to zoom in (down scale) from a Formation level to a Well level, incorporation more details:

# The lamination may give rise to stiffness contrasts, also laterally. Stiffer layers attract stresses in case of lateral compaction, whereas softer (and usually also weaker) layers get shielded from stress build up. Especially the stress build up by depletion (useful for infill drilling) can be predicted better. Incorporating the stiffness contrasts in a zoom in model around a well (snake mesh) improves the stress prediction.

# Well stability predictions are usually derived from well log correlations, where stiffness and strength parameters have been correlated to porosity (or Vp) and Surface Area measurements. Lab measurements for rocks like shale are rare.

# there is need to model borehole scale problems, like effects of cooling by injection on near-well bore stresses, including the effect of casing and cement.

# the user want to perform a Casing Integrity study, relating rock deformation to steel deformation and RTCM (casing strain measurement) data

The envisaged use of the zoom-in option is as follows.

1.     A user completes a full 3D mesh as usual and performs a calculation with upscaled (averaged) parameters.

2.     Prior or after the FEM calculation, the user imports existing or projected wells by means of a well path. The user can now project Results of the FEM calculation on the well.

3.     The user creates a zoom-in model of the well for a certain interval. The user specifies a certain range in AHD, for which he needs a zoom in model and an along-hole mesh density. These two determine the number of elements in vertical direction.  The most practical way is to provide a start- and end Depth, within the Geomec range.

4.     Calculation Results (Linear and/or Nonlinear after choice of user) from the full Geomec run are transferred as boundary conditions and initial stress data and so are the Material Models (constitutive law) and Parameters, fluid pressures and temperatures per depletion stage.

5.     The user imports log-data on the well coordinates from a LAS file into the Data-section. The relation is on AHD (=Along Hole Depth =MD =Measured Depth), which should be tied to Geomec calculated AHD (on nearest neighbour principle). The properties can be in different unit systems, like m/s or microsecond/ft, where the most frequent obvious ones should be recognized and converted to Geomec (SI) units. The following data should be recognized and extracted from the file:

a.      DEPT:  AHD in M or FT   (to: m)

b.     RHOB: Density in K/M3, ppg or g/cc  (to: kg/m3)

c.      NPHI:  porosity (always dimensionless)

d.     YME: Young’s Modulus  in Pa, MPa, psi  (to:  MPa)

e.      DT:  Transit time (= 1/Vp) in us/m or us/ft   (to: Vp in m/s)

f.       VP:  acoustic velocity in m/s or ft/s  (to Vp in m/s)

6.     Correlations will be preprogrammed in the calculator to determine Friction angle, Young’s Modulus, cohesion from the values above. The user can change formulas to liking.

7.     The user drags (where required) the (1D) pointset parameters or Calculator Parameter to the mesh sections (Formations) to overwrite the Geomec values.

8.     The snake mesh can be rerun to derive results with downscaled parameters.

9.     The stress data and material properties can be used to start up packages as Stabor, FIST or Casint

 

Casing Integrity, RTCM and heat/fluid flow around a well

 

With the zoom-in model, the user can further zoom-in to Casing scale, usually restricted to a length of less than 30 metres and a diameter of less than 20 metres. The Casing model contains elements with the a thickness equal to the wall thickness, which is usually in the order of 1 cm. This limits the size of all elements and hence the volume that can be dealt with.

 

The casing model requires a steel model, which is a table relating stress and plastic (von Mises) strain, which is the general stress-strain curve from a general extension (pulling) test, corrected for the elastic strain contribution.

 

The cement is seen as a rock model, where the user can select elastic or plastic (Mohr-Coulomb, Camclay) behaviour.

 

The casing model will take stress and deformation from its parent model, but the user my replace these by putting stress tensors and deformation vector pointsets to the Boundary Conditions (per depletion stage).

The user may also replace material models, pore pressures, temperatures for the rocks.

 

The Casing itself needs an internal fluid pressure and temperature, which can either act as a boundary condition for the casing or as a start for a Heat Flow or Mixture (fluid) flow run. In case of fluid flow, the user needs to indicate whether the casing is perforated or not, setting the permeability of the casing (steel) to infinity or zero.

 

By default, the non-linear run for casing integrity is performed using non-linear geometry, updating the position of every node per step, hence taking into account effects of ovalization or hole size reduction. Non-linear geometry can be turned off by unchecking Large Deformations in the Nonlinear Calculation Parameters dialog.
