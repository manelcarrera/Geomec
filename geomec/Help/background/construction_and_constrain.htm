Model construction and constraints  

Hexahedron type model

For a hexahedron type model the finite element model is constructed from the horizons, the element-grid defined in the main mesh region and possible sub-mesh regions and the number of elements over the thickness of each formation. The following assumptions apply:

  • Every horizon stretches over the full horizontal domain of the model. If a horizon is defined via a horizon-file and does not cover the full horizontal domain of the model, the depths at the outer edges of the specified area are extrapolated with constant depth over the horizontal domain of the model.

  • Horizons have a strict sequence of depth, which is defined by the user.

  • Horizons do not intersect and can also coincide in total or partly. If at a certain location the vertical distance between 2 horizons is less than 1 meter, locally the depth of the lower horizon is defined by the depth of the upper horizon plus 1 meter.

In GEOMEC 3D the finite element grid is structured and built from eight-noded brick elements. The user can specify a rectangular grid (parallel to North and East direction) in the horizontal plane. Zones with mesh refinement can be defined in this grid. In vertical direction the number of elements over the thickness of the formation is constant for every formation and the user can specify this number. The elements share nodes with neighboring elements in the same layer so that the horizons are always modeled as continuous surfaces. If the horizon is defined via a surface-file, the depth of the nodes on a horizon are defined as sample points of the grid on this surface. The depths of the nodes inside a formation are divided equally between the depth of the top and the bottom of the formation. The nodes at the bottom surface are supported in the vertical direction. The nodes at the vertical outer surfaces are supported in the normal direction. The nodes at the top-surface of the model as well as all the internal nodes are free to move in any direction.
